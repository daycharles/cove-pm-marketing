## Executive impact

The PDF is a stronger product source than the current generic multifamily positioning for near-term
lead generation. It narrows the first commercial wedge to inspection workflow + make-ready / field
coordination, with housing authorities and affordable housing operators as the provisional primary
ICP. Generic multifamily managers remain a secondary expansion lane until a real opportunity proves
otherwise.

## Confirmed or implementation-backed story

- Connected property-operations software for clearer work, faster decisions, and accountable execution.
- Inspection workflow and make-ready execution are the best-supported initial entry points.
- Work assignment, scheduling, bulk actions, timelines, attachments, messages, and audit history are
  implementation-backed surfaces according to the PDF's validation section.
- Reporting includes tenant-scoped reports, CSV export, and scheduled delivery according to the
  implementation validation summarized in the PDF.
- The most reusable workflow narrative is: **inspection findings -> accountable make-ready work ->
  clear ready state**.

## Still not safe to claim

- Route optimization or dispatch behavior.
- Production AI or autonomous Autopilot outcomes.
- Offline mobile behavior.
- Named production integrations.
- Security certifications, public-sector compliance, SLAs, deployment timelines, or customer proof.
- Quantified performance outcomes.

## Lead-generation decision

The first account list should favor public housing authorities and affordable-housing operators with
visible inspection, maintenance, make-ready, or contractor-coordination workflows. The first message
should ask about one measurable workflow, not sell a complete property-management platform.

## Qualification questions for every account

1. What inspection or make-ready workflow is currently fragmented?
2. Which roles own inspection findings, assignment, completion, and approval?
3. What is the current volume and baseline time to assign or complete?
4. What evidence must be captured for audit, owner, HUD, or resident reporting?
5. What data, spreadsheet, export, or integration is required for a pilot?
6. Who owns procurement and product/security review?

## Source review notes

The ten-page PDF is clearly structured as an evidence boundary and product-marketing source of truth,
not as an approved public brochure. It explicitly labels hypotheses and unknowns, calls for product-
owner confirmation, and identifies security, integration, implementation, and proof-point backlogs.
That makes it safe for research and pilot qualification, but not sufficient by itself for public claims.
