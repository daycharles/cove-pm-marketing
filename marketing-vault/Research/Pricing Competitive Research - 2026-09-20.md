# Competitive Pricing Research — 2026-09-20

Status: provisional market input, not a Averion Compass pricing decision.

## Executive read

Public competitors use three recognizable patterns:

1. Transparent monthly tiers with feature gates and add-on/transaction fees.
2. Per-unit pricing with minimums or small-portfolio floors.
3. Quote-led pricing for larger or more complex portfolios.

This supports testing a simple Averion Compass structure during the pilot, but it does not support
publishing a final price before we understand the pilot's measurable value, onboarding burden,
portfolio size, and required services.

| Buildium | Essential from $62/month; Growth from $192/month; Premium from $400/month | Tiered monthly plans, with payment, screening, inspection, e-signature, and other add-on or transaction fees | Clear low-entry anchor, but total cost depends on usage and plan |

| AppFolio | Core, Plus, and Max tiers are quote-led; a 50-unit minimum is stated for Core | Quote-led scaled platform with minimums and tiered capabilities | Benchmark for larger operators and value-based selling |
| Rent Manager | Public page presents bundles and additional services but does not expose a simple universal base price in the retrieved content | Bundle/quote conversation | Useful reminder to compare implementation and services, not only subscription price |

## Pricing implications for Averion Compass

- Remove unverified public prices from the website until the pilot and packaging are approved.
- Keep the first offer easy to explain: one scoped pilot, clear baseline, defined success metrics,
  and an explicit conversion path.
- Test a pricing hypothesis with a monthly floor plus a per-unit component for the full platform,
  while separately identifying implementation, migration, payments, screening, and premium-service
  costs.
- Avoid claiming to be the cheapest option. Compete on measurable maintenance operating value,
  clarity of ownership, evidence, and fewer disconnected workflows.
- Ask prospects what they pay today, including software, staff time, vendor coordination, payment
  fees, add-ons, onboarding, and switching cost. Subscription price alone is not the comparison.

## Pilot pricing questions

- Is the pilot no-cost, discounted, or paid? Who approves that commitment?
- What portfolio size and number of properties are included?
- Is the pilot maintenance-only or does it include resident, vendor, reporting, or other workflows?
- What onboarding, migration, support, and data-export work is included?
- Which measurable result earns conversion: time to assignment, overdue-work rate, staff touches,
  repeat repair, vendor response, or resident update compliance?
- What conversion price and minimum commitment would be acceptable after value is demonstrated?
