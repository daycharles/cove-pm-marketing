## Decision question and boundary

**Question:** Which private multifamily operators deserve the first *discovery research* slot when regional workflow fit and an appropriate corporate/technology route matter more than mere vendor registration or portfolio size?

**Target:** Regional multifamily operations at Drucker + Falk and WinnCompanies/WinnResidential, subject to route and product-evidence review. **Buyer hypothesis:** regional operations or maintenance workflow owner, with IT/property-technology evaluator and procurement identified through that owner; no named individual is a qualified buyer. **Problem hypothesis:** inspection findings may lose context between follow-up assignment and unit-turn review. This is an interview hypothesis, not an observed problem at either operator. Asking how the operator handles findings, accountable work and sign-off is not a claim that Averion Compass currently delivers complete production sign-off; approved positioning requires release-accepted role-specific proof before any technical answer or demonstration.

**Method:** Official pages rechecked on **2026-10-02**; their undated page content is a current observation, not proof of a dated procurement event. The 2026-09-30 [landscape](Private%20Multifamily%20RFP%20and%20Exposure%20Landscape%20-%202026-09-30.md) ranks ten operators: Fairfield, MG, Winn, RPM, Gables, Weidner, Bell, BH, Cortland and Greystar; its first bounded-discovery subset is Fairfield/MG/Winn/RPM/Weidner/Gables. The [exposure plan](../Plans/Private%20Multifamily%20RFP%20Exposure%20and%20Discovery%20Plan%20-%202026-09-30.md) prioritizes Drucker + Falk/FPI for regional discovery *research*, Bell/Bozzuto for buyer-path research, Greystar for RFP consideration and Avenue5 for differentiation. Its existing local account map is not an operator relationship, contact access or verified regional workflow. These are different research lenses, not competing reports of buying intent. No live software RFP or operator interest was verified.

## Reconciled ranking recommendation (internal research, not permission to contact)

**Comparison as of 2026-10-02:** This orders *research steps*, not operators by demonstrated workflow fit or likelihood to buy. For Winn, the published general-information email is accessible, but a specific region, inspection/turn workflow, technology evaluator and appropriate software route are all unverified; next check the business suitability of the email and role/region evidence. For Drucker + Falk, an older local regional-scale hypothesis exists but current region/workflow evidence and buyer authority are unverified, and the extracted contact destination is incomplete; next inspect the official contact destination and role map. Fairfield has an operator-published Ohio portfolio signal but only a service-vendor route; Gables describes IT/vendor work and facilities but offers an owner-facing inquiry, not software supplier intake. FPI's current decision rights are unverified. Thus the first two are conditional *research slots* based on differing evidence gaps, not qualified leads or proven superior regional fit.

1. **Winn, then Drucker + Falk for source review** — Winn's published general-information email warrants verification first, but reachability is not buyer-route clarity or approval to email. The existing approval-gated drafts and local account map are internal artifacts, not company engagement or evidence of current workflow need. Drucker + Falk remains conditional until its contact and regional/buyer evidence is refreshed. Neither has confirmed software eligibility.
2. **Fairfield, then Gables** — Fairfield is a bounded Ohio portfolio hypothesis but needs a non-credentialing corporate path; Gables has a named IT function and facilities context but its owner form is wrong for software suppliers. Advance either only when corporate evaluator routing is validated. **MG, RPM and Weidner** remain research backups: MG's apparent corporate email/form points to resident care; RPM's displayed form is broken and its named addresses serve other purposes; Weidner offers only generic vendor inquiries.
3. **Bozzuto, then Bell** — map regional workflow/IT buyer and appropriate business path before any external drafting; Bell's vendor enrollment is closed and service-oriented. **FPI** drops from the 2026-09-30 first wave pending official FPI/Asset Living relationship and decision-rights evidence, not because a change of buying authority was shown. **Avenue5** remains a later differentiation test, not an initial route win.
4. **Greystar** — separate strategic *RFP-readiness research* lane, strongest general supplier RFP-consideration signal **among reviewed routes** but not first small-workflow discovery account. Software category/eligibility and actual requirements remain unknown; do not submit the form.

**Why the lists differed:** the landscape rewarded bounded portfolios and publicly visible entry points even when those were contractor/vendor portals; the exposure plan used an internal account map and regional/buyer *hypotheses*, not evidenced operations relationships. Neither score turns a general form or email into technology procurement; FPI's branded-domain finding introduces unresolved identity/decision-rights questions, not proof of changed authority. The recommendation prioritizes closing evidence gaps over rank by unit count alone; buyer-route clarity has not yet been established for either first slot.
