## Commercial objective

Find company-level housing authorities and affordable-housing operators with visible inspection,
make-ready, maintenance, or field-coordination workflows, while keeping the first outreach list
small enough for evidence review and human-approved contact.

## Decision

Prioritize public housing authorities and affordable-housing operators first because the product
source identifies them as the provisional primary ICP and names inspection workflow plus make-ready
execution as the best-fit entry point. Keep third-party multifamily managers as a secondary lane,
and keep very large platforms as strategic lighthouse accounts rather than assuming they are the
fastest first sale.

## Buyer hypothesis

Start with the agency or regional operations owner: housing-authority operations/procurement lead,
public-housing modernization or inspections lead, affordable-housing COO/VP Operations, regional
operations leader, maintenance/facilities leader, or innovation/technology owner. Do not collect
personal contact details until the company-level fit and business path are approved.

## Why this segment

- Maintenance coordination is repeated across many communities and regions.
- The value story can be measured through time to first response, time to assignment, overdue work,
  vendor response, repeat repairs, turn readiness, and resident updates.
- Public and affordable-housing operators have visible inspection, habitability, compliance, and
  make-ready workflows where evidence and handoffs matter.
- Third-party managers remain a useful expansion lane because they have a business incentive to show
  operational consistency to multiple owners.
- Regional or workflow-specific pilots are more plausible than a national platform rollout on a
  first touch.

## Primary queue: public and affordable housing

The guarded qualification report is at `agent-workbench/outputs/lead-qualification-primary.md`.

1. **New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA)** — qualified for review based on annual inspections,
   minor-repair/follow-up workflow, and public maintenance/inspection procurement signals.
2. **Chicago Housing Authority (CHA)** — qualified for review based on property-management contracts
   that include maintenance, repairs, and inspections.
3. **Housing Authority of the City of Los Angeles (HACLA)** — strong evidence fit, but confirm the
   operational volume and buying authority for a narrowly scoped pilot.
4. **FPI Management** — affordable-housing operator with explicit maintenance and owner-reporting
   signals; likely the most approachable non-government pilot candidate.
5. **WinnCompanies / WinnResidential** — large affordable-housing operator with maintenance,
   compliance, and asset-preservation functions; research a regional or portfolio entry point.
6. **National Church Residences** — affordable senior-housing operator; validate a maintenance or
   make-ready workflow before outreach.
7. **Philadelphia Housing Authority (PHA)** — clear inspection-data workflow, but current operational
   pain and decision path need more evidence.
8. **Asset Living** — strategic affordable-housing expansion account; current evidence supports fit,
   but its scale suggests a longer enterprise path.

## Secondary queue: multifamily expansion

1. **BH Management Services** — strong fit because BH publicly describes leasing-to-maintenance
   operations, in-house processes, and a technology/data orientation.
2. **RPM Living** — high-value scaled operator with 240,000+ units, 5,000+ professionals, and 15
   regions; likely longer cycle, but a credible strategic account.
3. **Willow Bridge Property Company** — large regional operating footprint and clear regional
   management structure; investigate one region or client portfolio rather than pitching nationally.
4. **Avenue5 Residential** — smaller and more reachable than the largest national managers; test a
   hands-on, customized operations message, with current unit scale revalidated before outreach.
5. **Greystar** — influential lighthouse account with massive operating complexity; research only
   until a warm path or narrowly scoped regional use case is found.

## Falsifiable pilot hypothesis

For a selected regional or portfolio operations team, Averion Compass can make maintenance work easier to
triage, assign, schedule, complete, and communicate by giving each request a clear owner, next
action, history, and evidence trail. The pilot succeeds only if baseline and post-pilot measures
show improvement in agreed operational metrics; no performance outcome is claimed in advance.
