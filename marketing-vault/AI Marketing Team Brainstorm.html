The working thesis

CovePM should not begin with a collection of autonomous agents chatting freely. It should begin with a local marketing operating system: a shared evidence graph, narrowly scoped agents, explicit goals, durable action records, and human approval before external or commercial actions.

CovePM Marketing OS = a local marketing graph with goal-owned AI agents, evidence-linked content, and a human approval control room.This is a design hypothesis, not a final architecture decision.

Target

US property-management operators, starting with urgent maintenance workflow problems and the buyer who owns operational performance.

Commercial outcome

Generate qualified demo conversations and measured pilot opportunities without overclaiming product capability.

Operating principle

Every recommendation should identify its evidence, confidence, owner, next action, metric, and approval state.

What Graphed contributes

Graphed presents marketing as connected data plus specialized agents, not simply prompting. Its site describes live source connections, a warehouse, goal-scoped agents, logged actions, dashboards, and an MCP interface for querying the data. Graphed overview · Graphed MCP

Patterns worth borrowing

  • Agent library: reusable specialists for channels and outcomes.
  • Owns + goal: every agent has a bounded surface and a measurable objective.
  • Action ledger: recommendations and changes are queryable after the fact.
  • Control room: humans see activity, exceptions, and performance in one place.
  • Shared data substrate: agents, dashboards, and assistants work from the same model.

What not to copy blindly

  • Cloud-first hosting when the initial requirement is local and isolated.
  • Autonomous ad-budget, outreach, or publishing actions before controls exist.
  • Unverified performance examples as planning assumptions.
  • A massive connector surface before the first workflow is reliable.
Local translation: Obsidian Markdown remains the human-readable source of truth; SQLite/Postgres stores structured state and action history; LangGraph orchestrates workflows; Ollama runs local models; Jira is a scoped work-management adapter.

Five ideas from the supplied PDF

The PDF adds practical “small agents” that are more immediately useful than a grand autonomous crew. Each can be implemented as a bounded workflow with source links and explicit uncertainty.

01 · Competitive intelligence

Competitor watch

Scan selected competitor channels on a schedule, filter for product fit and unusually strong performance, then compare findings with the roadmap and backlog.

CovePM adaptation:

Track maintenance, resident communication, vendor workflow, and property-operations themes. Create a research note or Jira task only when the insight is actionable.

02 · Execution hygiene

Commitment tracker

Extract decisions, owners, deadlines, and status from meeting notes or approved communications. Flag missing ownership instead of guessing.

CovePM adaptation:

Connect it first to session notes and the vault, then optionally to approved Slack or meeting sources. Keep a durable commitment ledger separate from marketing copy.

03 · Content leverage

Repurposing writer

Turn one verified product or campaign update into channel-specific drafts while preserving every fact, date, and number.

CovePM adaptation:

One approved maintenance update could yield a website section, email draft, LinkedIn post, sales talking point, and pilot follow-up. Missing facts become review questions.

04 · Cross-system safety

Change review agent

Check a product, website, or documentation change against related systems and flag verified dependency risks before release.

CovePM adaptation:

Compare product changes with positioning, website claims, demos, screenshots, and agent prompts. This is especially useful when features or integrations change.

05 · Message integrity

Content freshness

Compare published pages and third-party mentions against the current product and pricing source of truth. Flag only specific mismatches.

CovePM adaptation:

Run quarterly, and immediately after product or pricing changes. It should produce a correction queue, not silently edit public pages.

Synthesis

These are capabilities, not five more bots

The best design may be a shared platform where each capability uses common services: source retrieval, evidence scoring, task creation, approval checks, and action logging.

Architecture brainstorm

Vault + source data
Evidence graph
LangGraph workflows
Approval queue
Artifacts + metrics

Knowledge graph

Segments, personas, pains, claims, sources, features, competitors, messages, and proof points.

Why: gives agents durable context and makes claim provenance visible.

Operations graph

Tasks, agent runs, approvals, artifacts, commitments, experiments, owners, status, and outcomes.

Why: makes the system accountable and compatible with Jira-like taskboards.

Action ledger

Every run records agent, task, inputs, sources, proposed action, actual action, approval state, and result.

Why: supports auditability, debugging, and “what changed because of the agent?” reporting.

Control room

One local dashboard for active work, evidence gaps, stale content, commitments, experiments, and approvals.

Why: turns agent activity into an operational view instead of a stream of opaque transcripts.

Example end-to-end flow

  1. A Jira or vault task says: “Draft a maintenance-focused page for multifamily operators.”
  2. The router identifies the segment, required evidence, and relevant source notes.
  3. Market Intelligence retrieves buyer language and competitor patterns.
  4. Website Messaging creates a draft grounded in CovePM's positioning.
  5. Freshness / Change Review checks claims against the product reference.
  6. Human approval is required before public site changes or outreach.
  7. The artifact, sources, reviewer, decision, and metric are logged.

Possible implementation path

Phase 1

Evidence and task foundation

  • Define schemas for findings, claims, artifacts, approvals, commitments, and agent actions.
  • Read/write the existing vault.
  • Use mock Jira tasks first.
Phase 2

Three-agent pilot

  • Market Intelligence
  • Website / Content
  • Experiment Analyst
  • Add action ledger and review queue.
Phase 3

Sandbox integrations

  • Connect isolated Jira project.
  • Add website and analytics read access.
  • Add commitment and freshness checks.
Phase 4

Approved channel actions

  • Repurposing pipeline.
  • Lead research and CRM drafts.
  • Optional publishing connectors with explicit approval.
Recommended first success test: process five real or synthetic marketing tasks end to end, with source-linked outputs, no unsupported claims, a human approval step, and a complete action record.

Open questions and ideas not yet resolved

Product and scope

  • Is the first objective demo generation, pilot conversion, content velocity, or marketing learning?
  • Which segment gets priority: multifamily, residential, affordable, student, commercial, or associations?
  • What is the smallest proof point we can measure without a live customer?
  • Should the system manage only CovePM marketing, or eventually become a reusable platform?

Data and privacy

  • Which sources are safe to connect locally, and which must remain export-only?
  • Will any personal or prospect data enter the system? If so, what retention and suppression rules apply?
  • Do we need a separate sanitized research store?
  • How will source freshness and data-quality failures be detected?

Agent design

  • Should agents be organized by role, channel, outcome, or a hybrid?
  • Which decisions can be automated, recommended, or only drafted?
  • When should an agent stop and ask for clarification?
  • How do we evaluate an agent beyond “the writing sounds good”?

Research quality

  • What counts as strong evidence for a buyer pain or competitor insight?
  • How do we distinguish an outlier post from normal competitor activity?
  • How do we handle blocked pages, dynamic websites, and unreliable engagement counts?
  • Should every claim get a confidence score and expiry date?

Measurement

  • What is the canonical source for website conversions, demos, pilots, and pipeline?
  • How will we attribute an opportunity to research, content, outreach, or an experiment?
  • What does “agent improvement” mean: quality, speed, conversion, cost, or fewer errors?
  • What dashboard should be reviewed daily versus weekly?

Operational resilience

  • What happens when the local model is unavailable or gives malformed output?
  • Do we need approval expiry, rollback, and idempotency for every external action?
  • How will prompts, model versions, schemas, and tool permissions be versioned?
  • Should there be a kill switch for all agents and a per-agent pause control?

Additional ideas to consider

  • Proof-point miner: find measurable evidence in demos, support notes, and pilots.
  • Message drift detector: compare website, sales materials, and agent outputs for contradictions.
  • Interview synthesizer: turn customer conversations into anonymized themes and follow-up questions.
  • Offer / pilot configurator: draft scope and baseline plans without committing pricing or outcomes.
  • Opportunity radar: connect research signals to specific backlog tasks and experiments.

Decisions to defer

  • Whether to use CrewAI instead of LangGraph.
  • Whether to adopt Graphed Cloud or build a complete warehouse immediately.
  • Whether to add paid media optimization.
  • Whether to permit autonomous outbound communication.
  • Whether to build a full visual control room before the workflows prove useful.

Current recommendation

Build the smallest version that proves the operating model: a local evidence graph, three agents, a review queue, and a queryable action ledger. Let the agent library grow from observed bottlenecks rather than designing every channel up front.

Next brainstorm choice: decide whether to sketch the data model first, design the control-room dashboard, or define the first three agent workflows in detail.
