# Private Multifamily RFP Exposure and Discovery Plan

**As of:** 2026-09-30  
**Owner:** Marketing team  
**Status:** Research and plan complete; execution gates remain  
**Focus:** Private multifamily operators; social media is deferred.

## Objective and working hypothesis

Earn invitations to relevant private-operator software evaluations by becoming known to regional operations and maintenance leaders, property-technology / IT evaluators, and procurement teams before an RFP is issued. Use one specific, evidence-backed inspection-to-make-ready workflow to start the conversation, then qualify the buying process and prepare for a scoped evaluation.

**Hypothesis:** A regional operator will be more willing to consider Averion Compass when the team first demonstrates a grounded understanding of a real inspection, maintenance, or unit-turn handoff problem and can answer workflow, product, implementation, and security questions precisely. This is a hypothesis to validate, not an established market result.

## What the research indicates

- Formal private-operator RFPs can be selective and relationship-led. Greystar explicitly invites suppliers to register interest in upcoming National Accounts RFPs; it says preferred/recommended suppliers are chosen through a rigorous RFP and separate supplier compliance is required before serving communities. The intake route is therefore a useful signal and route to consideration, not evidence of an active bid or an assurance of an invitation.
- NAA’s 2026 supplier guidance describes a crowded field and advises suppliers to research the operator’s software context, respect the chain of command, and use relevant, personable contact. Its operator/technology coverage emphasizes identifying who benefits, understanding mission and values, giving implementation and training attention, and defining how pilot success will be measured.
- NAA’s supplier programs, events, exhibit opportunities, and partner channels are visibility options, but their price and audience fit need a separate review before any spend. This plan proposes no paid participation.
- Compass positioning in the current vault is inspections and unit turns: record findings, assign follow-up work, and track a unit through review/sign-off. No customer outcomes, named integrations, security certifications, SLAs, deployment timelines, or quantified results are verified in the source materials. Keep those as explicit RFP gaps until evidence is supplied.

## Target and buyer map

Start with owner/operators and third-party property managers with regional operating teams and visible multifamily operations. Prioritize a bounded region or workflow over enterprise-wide replacement. Initial buyer map:

1. **Workflow champion:** regional operations or maintenance leader, director of maintenance, or regional property operations leader.
2. **Operational evaluator:** property manager, maintenance supervisor, inspection / make-ready process owner.
3. **Technical evaluator:** property technology, IT, integrations, data, or security lead.
4. **Commercial gatekeeper:** procurement / strategic sourcing, finance, and legal; involve them after a real use case and buying path are confirmed.
5. **Executive sponsor:** VP / COO / portfolio leader when the workflow spans regions or needs budget sponsorship.

Use official company pages, leadership directories, supplier forms, association programs, public presentations, job postings, and published case studies to map roles. Record business contact routes and source URLs only after account qualification; do not collect personal contact details without a validated company fit and approved business path.

## Exposure and discovery motions

1. **Create a useful RFP readiness page / brief.** Prepare a concise Compass overview centered on inspections and unit turns, with a workflow diagram, current-release screenshots, clearly bounded capabilities, implementation questions, and evidence gaps. No unsupported outcome claims. Keep the Facebook page separate and out of this motion.
2. **Earn operator-specific familiarity.** Review each company’s public portfolio, operating model, region, technology context, values, current transformation signals, and public evaluation routes. Write a one-page account brief with “why now” evidence and a falsifiable problem hypothesis.
3. **Enter through the right level.** Begin with regional operations / maintenance or the workflow owner. Learn who owns the software evaluation and how a vendor becomes eligible. Do not bypass the operator’s chain of command or blanket-message executives.
4. **Use structured discovery before pitching a pilot.** Ask how inspections become assigned work, who tracks make-ready state, where evidence and approvals live, which metrics are reviewed, what systems must connect, what the buying process looks like, and which implementation/security requirements are mandatory.
5. **Capture RFP signals.** Monitor official procurement/vendor pages, company news, public technology initiatives, role changes, portfolio transitions, event panels, operator presentations, and relevant job descriptions. Log evidence and date, then verify any opportunity against an official source before allocating response work.
6. **Build familiarity through high-trust channels.** Attend relevant local apartment-association / NAA sessions only where operator attendance and cost are justified; seek operator-led educational conversations and supplier ecosystem introductions. Treat directories and event access as routes to learn, not as endorsement or proof. Avoid paid sponsorship or exhibit commitments until cost, reach, and expected qualified conversations are reviewed.
7. **Respond selectively to a live RFP.** Run a bid/no-bid review against workflow fit, actual requirements, implementation capacity, data/security asks, schedule, buyer access, decision process, and commercial approval. Ask clarifying questions through the stated official channel. Do not represent uncertain capabilities as commitments.

## Reusable RFP response package

Marketing coordinates, product and engineering validate product statements, and the authorized company owner supplies corporate/legal/security evidence. Prepare these assets before submitting to any operator:

- Executive summary and product description for Averion Compass.
- Verified feature-to-requirement matrix, linked to the current product evidence; mark supported, roadmap/unknown, and out-of-scope distinctly.
- Inspection-to-make-ready workflow narrative and a short demo script using current-release behavior.
- Implementation approach with owner, responsibilities, dependencies, onboarding/training questions, and realistic timeline only after delivery confirms it.
- Integration / data-flow inventory: confirmed connections, export/import behavior, API status, data fields, ownership, retention, and unsupported needs. Do not claim integrations without product verification.
- Security and privacy questionnaire with only approved evidence: architecture, access control, encryption, backups, incident handling, subprocessors, retention/deletion, and any current independent reports or certifications. Route unknowns to security/legal; never infer a certification.
- Support model, service levels, business continuity, accessibility, insurance, references, and company financials only where verified and approved.
- Pilot design template: single workflow, property/region, users, duration, baseline, comparison measure, data access, success criteria, exit/conversion path, and procurement gates. No pricing, discount, price lock, SLA, outcome, or timeline is offered without approval.
- RFP response library with version/date, owner, source evidence, expiration/revalidation date, and approval status for every answer.

**Current readiness:** This document is a strategy plan, not a submission-ready RFP response. The local research explicitly records missing customer proof, quantified outcomes, named integrations, security certifications, SLAs, and deployment timelines. The operator’s requirements may uncover other gaps.

## Metrics and review

Track weekly, with counts separated into researched, qualified, approved, contacted, and observed outcomes:

- **Coverage:** target accounts with current evidence / six; target accounts with mapped champion, evaluator, and procurement route.
- **Readiness:** RFP library sections complete and validated / total; open critical product, security, implementation, and legal gaps.
- **Access:** official intake routes found; verified relevant operator conversations; invited vendor / shortlist status only when directly confirmed.
- **Learning:** discovery conversations completed; accounts with a confirmed pain, current process, baseline metric, decision owner, system requirements, timing, and procurement steps.
- **Commercial progression:** qualified opportunities, demo requests, active RFP invitations, bid/no-bid decisions, submissions, shortlist invitations, and win/loss reasons.
- **Efficiency:** hours per qualified account and RFP; content or event cost per qualified conversation if any paid activity is later approved.

Initial 30-day targets are process goals, not pipeline promises: 6 account briefs refreshed; 2 first-wave account selections; 1 claim/evidence register and RFP response skeleton reviewed; 2 qualified discovery conversations attempted only after approval; 0 unapproved sends or commitments. Report actual results and misses without converting them into implied market benchmarks.

## Risks and controls

- **RFP access is selective:** distinguish vendor registration from shortlist inclusion; concentrate on relationships and fit before formal bid windows.
- **Scale / cycle risk:** national platforms can have long cycles and demanding proof requirements; learn from Greystar’s intake, but first seek a bounded regional use case elsewhere.
- **Product proof gaps:** current evidence is narrow; do not claim full maintenance suite parity, integrations, certifications, ROI, or delivery promises.
- **No verified customer proof:** use workflow walkthroughs, product evidence, and measurable discovery; obtain approval before naming any customer or result.
- **Commercial / security commitments:** product, delivery, security, legal, and company leadership validate their domains; marketing does not improvise answers.
- **Outbound controls:** contact only through approved business routes after suppression, sender, message, and channel review. This plan authorizes no outreach.
- **Social priority:** keep social publishing paused while this plan is executed; the separate Facebook page can remain in progress without being used as an RFP substitute.
